# Customs Trade Law Skill

## When to Use

- Classifying a product under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS)
- Researching CBP ruling history for similar products
- Resolving conflicts between HQ and NY rulings
- Mapping CIT/CAFC case law to classification questions
- Building compliance analysis for import transactions

## How It Works

### Module 1: HTS Classification

**Input:** Product description (name, components, use, composition)

**Process:**
1. Identify candidate headings (4-digit level)
2. Apply General Rules of Interpretation (GRI 1-6)
3. Apply Additional U.S. Rules of Interpretation
4. Determine subheading (6-digit level typically)
5. Assign confidence score

**Output:**
- Primary classification with HTS number
- GRI analysis explaining why other headings were rejected
- List of alternative classifications considered
- Flagged controversies or unresolved issues

### Module 2: CROSS Ruling Research

**Input:** Product description or existing classification

**Process:**
1. Search CBP's CROSS database directly
2. Evaluate HQ vs NY authority (HQ overrides NY)
3. Trace revocation chains (rulings that were later revoked)
4. Assess evidence quality: verified rulings vs identifications vs internal advice

**Output:**
- List of applicable rulings with citation
- Authority level (HQ, NY, Chicago, etc.)
- Current status (current, revoked, modified, etc.)
- Summary of ruling facts vs your product

### Module 3: CIT/CAFC Case Mapping

**Input:** HTS number or product description

**Process:**
1. Find relevant judicial decisions
2. Extract holdings from slip opinion PDFs
3. Map precedent to classification risk
4. Identify cases that support or undermine proposed classification

**Output:**
- List of relevant cases with citations
- Key holdings relevant to your classification
- Risk assessment based on case law strength

## Limitations

- This skill is a research assistant, not a substitute for legal advice
- Classification determinations require physical examination of merchandise in some cases
- Rulings are persuasively binding on CBP but not precedential
- Case law analysis should be verified against current statute
